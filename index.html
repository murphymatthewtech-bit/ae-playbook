Account Playbook Generator

A tool I built for myself as a Mid-Market AE candidate — turns a company name into a sourced, structured deal-prep package: an account thesis, competitive battlecard, incumbent-tool detection, a stakeholder map, a MEDDPICC gap-checklist, an objection tree by persona, and channel-specific outreach.

Why I built this

Every AE application asks you to prove you can research an account and think through a deal before you've had one. Most candidates answer that with a written paragraph. I built a repeatable process instead — a set of instructions I gave to Claude that runs the same research-and-synthesis workflow for any named company, every time, sourced rather than guessed.

The output isn't just a research dump. It opens with an Account Thesis — my own read on why the account is worth pursuing, why now, the single strongest use case, the most likely reason it fails, and the recommended next action — because a pile of sourced facts with no point of view doesn't actually show how I'd work a deal.

It's not a deployed app that scrapes the web on its own — the research step (checking real job postings for tool signals, pulling competitor reviews, cross-referencing funding and product shape) needs real reasoning, so it runs inside a Claude session rather than as a canned script. Research tiers — thoroughness isn't the same as over-engineering

A mid-market AE role means high-volume outbound and short cycles — nobody should spend three hours researching every prospect. The workflow explicitly scales its own depth:

  • Tier 1 — five-minute brief. Standard outbound at volume: trigger event, likely pain, who to contact, one message angle, next action. Nothing more.
  • Tier 2 — twenty-minute meeting prep. Once a meeting is booked: snapshot, stakeholders, incumbent detection, discovery questions, one competitor comparison, a demo hypothesis.
  • Tier 3 — full opportunity playbook. Reserved for qualified or strategically important accounts (or a demonstration piece, like the example above): the complete nine-section structure.

How it works

  • Real research, not priors. Every non-obvious claim is sourced. Anything uncertain is explicitly flagged Inferred or Unknown, never stated as fact.
  • Incumbent detection. Checking a target's own current job postings for required tool experience — often the clearest public signal of what they already use or are evaluating.
  • Deal-specific battlecard. Not just competitor strengths/weaknesses, but likelihood they're the incumbent, likelihood they win, this account's specific wedge, and when not to compete.
  • MEDDPICC as an honest gap-checklist. Each element is tagged Verified / Inferred / Unknown with a next action — if there's no reliable evidence, it says "Unknown" rather than forcing an answer.
  • Discovery and outreach style follows Kevin Dorsey's problem-bucket-question, disarming-cold-open, and ask-for-interest-CTA approach (Live Better. Sell Better. / 30 Minutes to President's Club), split by channel (email, LinkedIn, call opener, multi-touch cadence) rather than one script pasted everywhere.
